howtall

Tap a tree and howtall tells you how tall it is, from the LiDAR survey Toitū Te Whenua LINZ flew over that spot. New Zealand only, wherever LINZ has a 1 m surface model.

Tree and Area

Tree gives the top of the tree you tapped: the highest point within 3 m of your tap, since a crown's top is rarely exactly where a finger lands. It also lists the tallest trees close by, so you can tell which one you meant.

Area reads a circle around the tap (10 to 50 m across the radius, so at most about 0.8 ha) and reports the tallest tree, the five tallest tree tops, how much of the circle is over 2 m, and the average height of that cover.

Where I am uses your phone's location. Under a forest canopy GPS is often out by 5 to 20 m, so it reports the tallest tree within your GPS accuracy, and the height right at your reported position.

How heights are worked out

Height is the surface model (the top of everything) minus the ground model (the bare earth under it), both from the same flight, on a 1 m grid. Things that are clearly power lines or pylons are left out: a cell far above everything round it and too narrow to be a crown. Buildings are not left out, so a tap on a roof gives the roof.

The 1 m grid can clip the very tip of a narrow, pointed crown (a young conifer, a cabbage tree), so the true top may be a metre or two higher. Heights are for the date the survey was flown; a tree may have grown since. When more than one survey covers the spot, the newest is shown first and the others are buttons under the result.

Data used

Nothing is downloaded until you tap. Then howtall reads just the 1 m cells around the spot from LINZ's Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs, typically a few hundred kilobytes to a couple of megabytes a tap, and reuses what it already has if you tap again nearby.
